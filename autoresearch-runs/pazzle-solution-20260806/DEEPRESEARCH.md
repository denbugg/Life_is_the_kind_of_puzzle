# Deep research — PAZZLE 576-tile reconstruction

## Benchmark reality

- PAZZLE is materially harder than standard square-jigsaw benchmarks: 576 tiles of only 20x20 pixels, with independent brightness/contrast shifts, Gaussian noise around 40-55/255, blur, and JPEG compression on every tile. The objective is final RGB SSIM, not only direct or neighbour placement accuracy.
- No public paper found evaluates this exact compound setting. Consequently there is no defensible single “SOTA number” to quote for PAZZLE. The closest scale match is Heck et al. (2025), which trains a transformer placement model on puzzles up to 600 fragments, but with larger 32/64-pixel pieces and erosion rather than the full compound degradation: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-025-01484-z
- A 2025 corruption benchmark confirms that clean-domain solver rankings collapse under corruption and that matched corruption fine-tuning matters; it evaluates up to 12x12 puzzles, still far below 24x24: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07828
- A Gaussian-noise result is unusually relevant: on 6x6 PuzzleCelebA with sigma 0.2 (about 51/255), classical Gallagher/MGC reached 97.75% direct accuracy, ahead of Pomeranz at 91.50% and GANzzle-ViT at 88.55%. This does not cover blur/JPEG/affine shifts or 576 pieces, but it proves that a classical gradient baseline must not be omitted: https://stuart-james.com/publications/2024PR-L-2/TalonPRL24.pdf

## Closest methods and transferable mechanisms

| angle | method | reported evidence | concrete transferable mechanism | caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A/B/F | Robust MGC/SSD compatibility | Selectively weighted MGC improves noisy/cropped-boundary compatibility; under one Gaussian setting SSD even beats MGC, while wMGC remains near the best across noise types: https://doi.org/10.1109/CRV.2013.54 | Compute normalized MGC+SSD at border depths 0/1/2 and use it as a confidence/rank correction, not as an uncalibrated dense blend. | Published noise/cropping is simpler than PAZZLE compound degradation. |
| J | Rank transplantation | The improved score could not be inserted directly because the assembler was sensitive to score geometry; swapping only the old/new top-1 scores preserved the original matrix scale: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261112081_Robust_Solvers_for_Square_Jigsaw_Puzzles | Keep the frozen base matrix; when CandidateSeamRanker/spatial head is sufficiently confident and reciprocal, transplant its top rank by swapping base scores. | Bad new top-1 choices are still destructive; sweep precision/coverage thresholds. |
| C/D | Sparse accurate anchors | Four to six correctly located, spatially spread anchors were enough to reconstruct puzzles with more than 400 patches in a graphical model: https://people.csail.mit.edu/billf/publications/A_Probabalistic_Image.pdf | Convert the existing 0.954-precision sparse edge set into anchor components and propagate context from them instead of treating all edges equally. | The cited anchors have absolute positions; PAZZLE starts with relative edge anchors, so component-to-grid positioning remains necessary. |
| G/C | Seeded message passing | SGMNet pools a large graph through reliable seeds, filters them, then unpools context; ablations identify seed-based pooling/unpooling as critical and robust to noisy seeds: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08771 | Run seed-conditioned attentional pooling over high-precision ranker edges, predict revised assignment costs, and retain weighted unpooling. | Feature matching is bipartite, not grid assembly; use as a score refiner, not a complete solver. |
| C | Learned multi-patch similarity | Directly learning a score from more than two patches outperforms aggregating pairwise similarities in multi-view matching: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Hartmann_Learned_Multi-Patch_Similarity_ICCV_2017_paper.html | Train a center-candidate score conditioned on 1-4 already anchored neighbours; this directly targets the local oracle R@1 jump from 0.1976 to 0.4528. | Requires honest training contexts with incorrect neighbours and missing-context masks. |
| C/E | Two-side verified block growth | Kafle and Banerjee begin with 2x2 blocks and merge only when at least two sides support the same block; they report near-perfect initial-block precision over more than 74% of sides on standard puzzles: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transfun/E109.A/2/E109.A_2025EAP1018/_pdf/-char/en | Enumerate top-k L-corners/2x2 blocks and accept a tile only when two calibrated neighbours agree; use DSU for block merging. | Earlier exact-loop search locally had almost zero coverage; top-k enumeration must pass a precision/coverage gate quickly. |
| C/E | Growing Consensus | Geometry/loop consensus reduces dependence on unreliable pair scores and reduced error by up to 75% on very small clean pieces: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2016/html/Son_Solving_Small-Piece_Jigsaw_CVPR_2016_paper.html | Require reciprocal edges and cycle-consistent 2x2 motifs before freezing an anchor component. | Local consensus/growth variants already failed; only the two-side/top-k formulation is sufficiently different to reopen. |
| D/E | Multi-phase relaxation labeling | Progressively anchors one piece/position at a time, guarantees a feasible permutation, restricts anchoring to the connected block, and uses alpha=0.7, epsilon=1e-4 in reported experiments: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14793 | Candidate structural backend after seed/context scoring; maintain a contiguous anchor block and branch only at dimension boundaries. | Dense 576x576 labeling can be expensive; prior local Sinkhorn/QAP failures make this a rung-3 experiment, not seed generation. |
| D/E | Robust LP assembly | Sequential LP relaxations remove inconsistent matches and fuse components; intended for global use of pairwise matches: https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2016/papers/paper139/index.html | Run only on sparse top-k=24-32 directional candidates after calibration. | Prior local global solvers failed; compute and integration cost are higher than two-side growth. |
| D/F | Transformer + Sinkhorn/Hungarian | Heck et al. use six transformer layers, d=1024, 16 heads, 320-D edge embeddings, 20 Sinkhorn iterations at tau=0.1 and Hungarian inference, and train up to 600 fragments: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-025-01484-z | Nearest scale-first structural model; use corruption-invariant tile/edge embeddings and a smaller d=384-512 variant. | No official code was found and current local absolute-position/DDPM branch is near chance. This is rung 3. |
| D/K | JPDVT | Diffusion ViT reports 75.9% piece accuracy on 150 pieces with 7% erosion versus 66.7% for SJP-ED; official code exists: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Liu_Solving_Masked_Jigsaw_Puzzles_with_Diffusion_Vision_Transformers_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf and https://github.com/JinyangMarkLiu/JPDVT | Use as a teacher/initialization or a smaller-grid structural baseline, not the first production solver. | 150 pieces and mismatched corruption; iterative inference is expensive at 576. |
| D/K | PuzzleFlow | 2026 work uses pretrained ViT fragment features plus position/time embeddings and four cross-piece transformer layers with discrete flow matching: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12077 and https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Shahar_The_Missing_GAP_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf | Structural reframe for holistic cross-piece reasoning; published training uses AdamW 1e-5, dropout 0.1, 30 epochs, AMP and 5/20 flow steps. | GAP-3/GAP-5 are tiny irregular puzzles; direct scaling to 576 is unjustified. |
| E | ERL-MPP local refinement | Scores 1x2, 2x1 and 2x2 configurations and refines with swap-2, swap-3 and swap-2x2; reports 70.9% absolute and 80.4% neighbour accuracy on MIT 10x15 gap=2: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09608 | Borrow deterministic swap neighbourhoods and multi-piece scoring after an initial solution. | At most 150 pieces; do not reproduce the full RL system initially. |
| B | Corruption-matched representation | Fine-tuning on matched corruption improves degraded-puzzle performance and can change the winning method: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07828 | Two independent corruption views per positive pair; curriculum edge dropout 0 -> 1 -> rarely 2 pixels; freeze assembler while measuring representation gains. | Benchmark corruption and tile sizes do not exactly match PAZZLE. |
| E | Eroded-boundary inpainting | Inpaint the missing seam and reuse the discriminator to classify whether two pieces are neighbours: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Bridger_Solving_Jigsaw_Puzzles_With_Eroded_Boundaries_CVPR_2020_paper.html | Optional seam-completion auxiliary head can force continuation reasoning beyond a single corrupted border row. | GAN training is expensive and boundary erosion differs from independent blur/JPEG/noise. |
| H | Degradation-conditioned restoration | FBCNN predicts JPEG quality and conditions its decoder, with dedicated double-JPEG augmentation: https://github.com/jiaxi-jiang/FBCNN and https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Jiang_Towards_Flexible_Blind_JPEG_Artifacts_Removal_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf | Predict per-tile quality/noise/blur/gain/bias and feed 24x24 condition maps through FiLM to the assembled-image restorer. Test oracle conditions first. | A 20x20 tile has little evidence for JPEG quality; abandon if oracle conditioning does not improve oracle-placement SSIM. |
| H/J | Exact metric loss | `skimage` SSIM depends on window, Gaussian weighting, covariance convention, channel axis and data range: https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/api/skimage.metrics.html#skimage.metrics.structural_similarity | Implement a differentiable replica of the exact competition call and verify numerical error below 1e-5 before fine-tuning restoration. | Secondary while placement is near random; SSIM-only loss can oversmooth or shift colour. |

## Cross-domain transplants

- **Seeded feature matching -> puzzle context (G):** SGMNet's sparse pool/filter/unpool design is a better match to the local high-precision/low-coverage evidence than a generic dense GNN. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08771
- **Multi-view stereo -> multi-neighbour seam scoring (G):** directly learned multi-patch similarity provides a concrete architecture for the observed context oracle gap. Source: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Hartmann_Learned_Multi-Patch_Similarity_ICCV_2017_paper.html
- **Forensic JPEG carving -> photometric nuisance handling (G):** orphaned JPEG fragments exhibit severe brightness/colour artifacts; the work jointly designs compatibility and stitching to mitigate them. Source: https://pkorus.pl/publications/2019-tifs-carving
- **Image stitching -> tile-wise colour correction (G/H):** colour-consistency optimization explicitly separates cross-image consistency from content fidelity, suggesting a jointly solved per-tile affine correction before seam scoring. Source: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2017_workshops/papers/w43/Xia_Color_Consistency_Correction_ICCV_2017_paper.pdf

## Known pitfalls and negative evidence

- Pair classification/ranking is not the target metric. PAZZLE already improved sampled pair accuracy to 0.477 without improving global placement; every experiment must end at solve-only/final SSIM on a fixed holdout.
- Direct score blending is unsafe when scorers have incompatible calibration. The robust-solver paper explicitly observed assembler sensitivity and used rank transplantation instead: https://doi.org/10.1109/CRV.2013.54
- Absolute-position models trained only at small puzzle sizes are a weak default. JPDVT, positional diffusion, and PuzzleFlow demonstrate mechanisms but not validated 24x24 scaling.
- Model selection on the same six scenes is optimistic. The current I21 gain comes from 28 alpha/budget choices on its reporting set; a new untouched gate is mandatory.
- Clean-puzzle leaderboard results are not evidence under compound corruption. Matched independent per-tile corruption views are part of the hypothesis, not optional augmentation: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07828

## Data notes

- Local data are already complete: 7,000 paired train inputs/targets and 700 test inputs under `E:/pazzle_data`; no external benchmark download is needed for the primary run.
- Use exact synthetic labels for training/evaluation. Recovered real-image permutations are noisy Hungarian pseudo-labels and should only provide optional weak data.
- Create three disjoint groups by source image, not only lexicographic filename: training; iterative validation; final untouched confirmation. Cache corruption draws and hashes so all candidate solvers see identical inputs.

## Research decision

The first batch should be training-free and end-to-end: establish the frozen holdout, reproduce corrected buddies and I21 on identical corruptions, test confidence-gated rank transplantation, multi-depth MGC/SSD rank correction, and two-side 2x2/DSU growth. Only after a verified transfer to solve-SSIM should compute be spent on corruption-invariant fine-tuning or seed-conditioned multi-context models. The full transformer/diffusion solvers are structural rung-3 options, not generation-0 defaults.
